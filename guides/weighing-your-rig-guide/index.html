Field notes

United Mobile RV sees overloaded axles and underrated tires on corridor calls more than owners expect. A proper weigh is cheap insurance compared to a blowout or frame stress.

How to weigh

CAT scale method

Drive on so each axle group sits on a pad. Record steer, drive, and trailer axles separately when the scale layout allows — totals alone hide which axle is hot.

As you travel

Weigh loaded the way you actually camp: full fresh or typical tanks, gear, passengers, and tow vehicle if applicable.

Tongue / pin weight

For trailers, tongue or pin weight is a large share of hitch and tow-vehicle rear axle load. Guessing it from “feels heavy” is not a measurement.

Read the ticket

Compare each axle to GAWR and the combination to GCWR/GVWR on the placards. Soft sidewalls or uneven tire wear often point at the hot axle.

When to book

DIY OK

Scale tickets and placard math are owner-doable. Bring a notepad photo of every weight label.

Call us when

You need help interpreting overload options, tire load ratings, or suspension/brake concerns after a weigh — Text with ticket photos.

Owner checklist
  • Weigh as loaded for a real trip, not empty
  • Photo every placard (GVWR, GAWR, tire)
  • Note tire load range and inflation cold
  • Re-weigh after major cargo or battery bank changes

Field reality on the corridor

Measure before you buy parts

Forums love parts lists. A meter, a scale ticket, or a cold pressure check usually beats a guess. Bring photos when you Text Now — year, make, and a clear shot of the fault beat a long thread.

DIY boundary

Owners can inspect, document, and do basic setup from manufacturer manuals. Stop when you smell burning, see structural damage, or the fix needs sealed systems, gas work, or high-current DC you are not trained for.

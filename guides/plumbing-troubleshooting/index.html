Field notes

Low pressure, a pump that won’t stop running, no hot water, or a sulfur smell — most RV plumbing complaints trace back to one of a handful of causes. Here’s how to narrow it down. BOOK A PLUMBING DIAGNOSIS Low Water Pressure Pressure loss almost always has one of these causes, in rough order of likelihood:

Water Pump Runs Constantly or Cycles Rapidly

A pump that won’t shut off almost always means there’s a leak somewhere downstream keeping the pressure switch from satisfying — the pump itself is rarely the actual problem.

No Hot Water

Check the bypass valve position before anything else — it’s the single most common cause and the easiest to miss.

Sewer Odor or Slow Drains

Sewer smell inside the RV is a venting or trap problem, not a tank problem — chasing the black tank won’t fix it.

Winterizing Basics

If you’re in a climate where temps drop below freezing, winterizing is not optional. Either blow out the lines with compressed air or use RV antifreeze — never automotive antifreeze.

When to Stop and Call a Professional

Leaks inside walls or under flooring, a pump that won’t hold prime after basic checks, or any sign of water damage on ceilings or floors are worth a proper diagnosis before they get worse.

Tires guide

RV tires age even when miles look low

Trailers and coaches often sit in sun and heat. Sidewall aging and date codes matter as much as tread depth — sometimes more.

Active corridor: Montana · Wyoming · Idaho · Washington — case-by-case beyond.

Field notes

United Mobile RV sees aged tires fail on hot pavement with “plenty of tread.” Treat calendar age as a safety limit.

What to inspect

DOT date code

Find the four-digit week/year. Manufacturer guidance varies — follow the tire maker and your coach/trailer guidance; do not invent a universal year cutoff here.

Sidewall cracks

Ozone checking and weather cracks on the sidewall are age/UV damage. Deep cracks are a replace-now signal.

Bulges & flat spots

Impacts and long sits create damage tread depth will not show.

Spare honesty

Spares age too. An ancient spare is not a plan.

Pressure still matters

Cold inflation

Set pressure cold for the actual load — see the load/pressure chart guide. Underinflation cooks aging sidewalls.

TPMS

A good TPMS catches heat and leaks early on corridor days.

Owner checklist
  • Read DOT date on every tire including spare
  • Inspect sidewalls in good light
  • Set cold pressure to load
  • Replace on age/damage even if tread remains

Field reality on the corridor

Measure before you buy parts

Forums love parts lists. A meter, a scale ticket, or a cold pressure check usually beats a guess. Bring photos when you Text Now — year, make, and a clear shot of the fault beat a long thread.

DIY boundary

Owners can inspect, document, and do basic setup from manufacturer manuals. Stop when you smell burning, see structural damage, or the fix needs sealed systems, gas work, or high-current DC you are not trained for.

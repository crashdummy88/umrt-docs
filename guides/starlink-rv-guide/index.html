Field notes

Clear sky view still wins outdoors — trees, canyon walls, A/C shrouds, and neighboring rigs beat any mount brand. Run the Starlink app obstruction check at the exact proposed height before you drill. A poorly sealed roof penetration is one of the most common sources of interior water damage, so this install is worth doing carefully. Dish + router draw meaningful continuous power; budget them with fridge and other always-on loads, not as negligible. OEM PDFs, app links, and support articles live on our software twin at software.unitedmobilerv.com/starlink/ — we point there instead of inventing watt numbers or plan fine print.

Roof-mount vs portable

Roof mount

Hands-off every time you park. Needs clear sky overhead and a sealed penetration done right — solid, level base into structure (not thin skin alone), and cable routing that does not create a water intrusion point. Not a job to rush. Highway vibration and freeze-thaw will find weak fasteners.

Portable dish

Flexible under trees; takes setup time and a secure cable run back to the coach. Useful when the roof position is shaded or you change sites often. Kickstand / picnic-table tests are fine for site checks — they are not a travel mount.

Obstructions beat mounts

Partial tree cover can look “fine” on a map and still kill uplink. If the app shows heavy obstruction at your usual parks, fix placement before blaming the router, Peplink, or the plan. Clear-sky field of view is the constraint that decides whether a permanent roof hole is worth it.

Optional Peplink

Want cellular failover or multi-WAN bonding alongside Starlink? We are a Peplink Certified Associate — that is the Peplink title we claim, nothing beyond it. Bonding and/or failover as configured: Hot Failover ≠ Bonding ≠ Smoothing. See the Peplink multi-WAN guide and /wireless/. Peplink does not invent clear sky.

Power: Mini vs Flat High Performance

Plan from official specification-sheet averages, not campground anecdotes. Field meters vary with temperature, throughput, obstruction hunting, and (on kits that heat) melt cycles — but undersizing from marketing talk is how inverters brown out and Mini USB-C paths reboot-loop.

Starlink Mini

Official sheet average about ~25–40 W. Lower continuous draw — the realistic citizen on modest lithium / solar. Still include it in daily watt-hour math with fridge and always-on loads. Undersized USB-C PD accessories cause reboot loops that look like “Starlink is down.”

Flat HP / High Performance

Official sheet averages about ~110–150 W — roughly three to four times Mini on paper. Treat as a real appliance on inverter, shore, or DC supply. Confirm continuous inverter rating, battery C-rate, cable gauge, and bay airflow before permanent install.

Off-grid budget

Include Starlink continuous draw in the daily watt-hour math. See the off-grid power budget guide before locking battery bank size. OEM PDFs indexed at /starlink/ on the software twin.

Fused integration

We route and fuse the feed so the install is part of the electrical system, not a dangling brick. Strain-relieve connectors; vibration opens mystery outages. On Victron coaches we treat Starlink as a known load in the energy budget.

Seals, cable, and when DIY ends

Seals & penetrations

Drip loops, chafe protection, grommets, and proper sealant at every roof penetration. A fast “temporary” hole becomes a soft floor two seasons later. Re-check fasteners and sealant after the first hard season — sun and freeze-thaw work on roof mounts.

Cable discipline

Use kit-correct cable. Shortest clean path; no crush in compartments or slides; strain relief at dish and interior. Label both ends. Zip-ties into soft membrane or pinched doors create “mysterious” dropouts later.

Heat and weather

Roof mounts see sun, ice, and highway miles. Cable jackets crack at chafe points first. Leave service access to inspect seals and connectors without tearing the install apart.

When DIY ends

New roof penetrations, sealed mounts, and coach DC integration. If the dish is offline after a move, start at Starlink offline troubleshoot before ordering another dish.

Owner checklist
  • Match Residential vs Roam to how you actually travel (fixed address ≠ full-time travel)
  • Assess clear-sky with the app at typical parks before committing to a permanent roof mount
  • Budget continuous dish + router draw from official averages (Mini ~25–40 W; Flat/HP ~110–150 W)
  • Inspect roof seals at the mount seasonally
  • Keep firmware / app updates from official Starlink sources (software twin indexes links)
  • If you also run cellular, plan antenna separation and name Peplink roles honestly: Hot Failover ≠ Bonding ≠ Smoothing

What good looks like on the road

After a stop

Roof mount: park, wait for acquire, confirm apps work before you unload the day. Portable: known cable path, secure footing, no trip hazard across the door.

Obstruction honesty

If the app shows heavy obstruction at your usual parks, fix placement before blaming the router or Peplink. Clear sky beats clever brackets every time.

One coach network

Phones hopping between Starlink Wi‑Fi and a separate hotspot is fragile. Peplink (or a deliberate single SSID design) keeps devices on one LAN while WANs change underneath — bonding and/or failover as actually configured.

Buying mistakes we see

Residential for a full-time travel coach. Flat HP on a marginal inverter because “bigger must be better.” Mounting under trees because the picnic table had signal once. Expecting Peplink or weBoost to compensate for a blocked sky.

FAQ

Residential vs Roam?

Residential = fixed address, no roam, no in-motion promise. Roam = travel / RV use, pause / standby, in-motion only in select areas. Starlink /rv redirects to Roam — not a separate SKU. We install hardware; plan choice stays with you and Starlink.

How much power off-grid?

Use official sheet averages: Mini ~25–40 W avg; Flat High Performance / High Performance ~110–150 W avg. Include continuous draw in the budget with other always-on loads. PDF index: software /starlink/.

Is roof-mount safe from leaks?

Only with a properly sealed penetration and cable routing. A rushed mount is a common water-intrusion source. Sealed roof penetrations matter as much as the dish itself.

Are you Starlink certified?

No. We are installs-only for Starlink — never “Starlink Certified.” Certified partner titles we do claim: Victron Professional Certified Installer, weBoost Authorized Installer, Peplink Certified Associate, Dometic Professional Certified.

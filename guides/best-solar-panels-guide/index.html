Best RV Solar Panels — Buying Guide

The “best” solar panel depends entirely on your roof space, mounting preference, and how the rig actually gets used — rigid, flexible, and portable panels each solve a different problem, and picking based on price alone usually means picking wrong.

Source: WP Field Guide. Corridor: Montana · Wyoming · Idaho · Washington — case-by-case beyond.

Field notes

Rigid vs. flexible vs. portable

Three panel families show up on the live guide. They are not interchangeable upgrades — each one is a different install.

Rigid

Typically aluminum-framed. Best efficiency and longest service life, but they add weight and need roof penetration points for mounting.

Flexible

Conform to curved surfaces and weigh less. Generally less efficient and shorter-lived than rigid. Lighter build is more prone to hot-spot damage without adequate airflow underneath. Fallback for curved or skoolie roofs where rigid will not mount — they run hotter and degrade faster.

Portable / folding

Move to follow the sun and do not require any roof mounting. Right call for occasional boondockers who do not want a permanent install; less so for full-timers who want max passive output.

Controller pairing

Flexible arrays on curved or skoolie roofs often pair with a Victron SmartSolar MPPT for Bluetooth-monitored charging. An undersized controller wastes real capacity even with premium panels.

Sizing before shopping

The right total wattage depends on your daily energy draw and available roof space, not a generic “more is better” rule. Work the math in the solar sizing & installation guide before choosing individual panels. Pair panel choice with the right charge controller — see MPPT vs PWM.

From the live Field Guide. Do not shop panels first and reverse-engineer the bank later. Roof space is a constraint, not the load. Daily watt-hours size the array; the bank gets you through night and cloud.

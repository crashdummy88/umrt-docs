Lithium & Solar · Guide

Best RV solar panels

The “best” panel is the one that fits the roof, the mounting method you will actually maintain, and the daily watt-hours you already calculated. Price-first shopping is how roofs get the wrong product.

Rigid framed modules win on efficiency and life. Flexible modules win on curved or weight-limited roofs and lose on heat and longevity. Portable kits win when you do not want a hole in the membrane. Pick the job, then the SKU.

Rigid vs flexible vs portable

Rigid (framed)

Aluminum-framed glass modules are the default rooftop choice. Best watts per square foot, longest service life, and replacement cells you can actually find. They need a rack or feet, and every foot is a penetration or a bond you must keep sealed.

Flexible

They conform to a crown or a skoolie curve and weigh less. They run hotter without airflow underneath, lose efficiency, and die earlier from hot spots and walk-on damage. Use them when rigid physically will not sit flat — not because they look cleaner in a photo.

Portable / folding

No roof work. You aim them at the sun and pack them when you roll. Right for weekend boondocking. Wrong as the only array for a full-time coach that needs passive harvest while you are gone.

Shade honesty

A/C shrouds, vents, and satellite dishes wreck series strings. One shaded cell can pull a whole series group down. Layout matters more than brand.

Size the system before you shop panels

Panel wattage is the last number, not the first. Work daily watt-hours in the sizing guide, then pick modules that fill the usable roof without self-shading.

Roof first

Measure usable flat area after A/C, vents, and antennas. Leave walk paths and seal access. A 400 W “deal” that does not fit is not a deal.

Controller match

Series strings want MPPT. See MPPT vs PWM. An undersized controller clips a premium array.

Owner checklist
  • Write down daily watt-hours before you add to cart
  • Map roof obstacles and shade at 10 a.m. and 3 p.m.
  • Decide rigid / flexible / portable for the roof you have
  • Match controller voltage and current to the array, not the other way around
  • Plan every penetration’s seal and drip loop before you drill

FAQ

More watts always better?

Not if the controller, wire, or roof cannot take them. Unused roof that shades itself is wasted money.

Can I mix old and new panels?

Only if voltage/current and age are close. Mixing leftover 12 V modules with a high-Voc string is how you cook a controller.

Flexible on a rubber roof?

Possible. Airflow and adhesive/fastener honesty matter. Expect a shorter life than framed glass.

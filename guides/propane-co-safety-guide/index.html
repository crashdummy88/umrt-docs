Safety guide

Propane and CO safety that keeps the coach livable

LP gas and carbon monoxide faults are not “sort it out tomorrow” projects. Detectors, ventilation, and leak response come before convenience.

Field notes

If you smell propane, hear a hiss, or a CO alarm sounds — get to fresh air and shut off the source. Do not keep troubleshooting in a closed coach.

Basics

Detectors

Test LP and CO detectors per maker guidance. Replace on expiration — an expired detector is a false sense of safety.

Leak response

No sparks, no stove lighting, ventilate, close tank valve, leave the space. Soap-solution checks are for cold systems by competent hands — not a lit match.

Appliances

Yellow tips, soot, or delayed ignition mean stop and diagnose. Incomplete combustion makes CO.

Storage

Cylinders upright and secured. Regulators and hoses are wear items — cracked hose is a replace-now.

Owner checklist
  • Test detectors before trips
  • Know where the tank valve is in the dark
  • Never ignore a CO alarm
  • Replace dated detectors on schedule

Field reality on the corridor

Measure before you buy parts

Forums love parts lists. A meter, a scale ticket, or a cold pressure check usually beats a guess. Bring photos when you Text Now — year, make, and a clear shot of the fault beat a long thread.

DIY boundary

Owners can inspect, document, and do basic setup from manufacturer manuals. Stop when you smell burning, see structural damage, or the fix needs sealed systems, gas work, or high-current DC you are not trained for.

FAQ

Can I wait until the next stop?

If brakes, tires, propane, or CO are involved, no — stabilize and get help. Cosmetic and planning items can wait.

Parts are quoted after diagnosis. We travel the corridor with common tools and meters; special-order parts are called out before work proceeds.

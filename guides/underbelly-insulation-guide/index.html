Field notes

Relevant reading before a Michigan winter, a U.P. run, or any cold-weather leg of a trip — most RVs are not built for it out of the box. Manufacturer four-season packages vary enormously — some add real heated, enclosed underbellies with tank heat pads; others add a thin layer of insulation and call it done. Knowing which you actually have before a cold trip matters more than the marketing label on the brochure.

“Four-Season” Doesn’t Mean What It Sounds Like

Manufacturer four-season packages vary enormously — some add real heated, enclosed underbellies with tank heat pads; others add a thin layer of insulation and call it done.

Tank Heat Pads & Skirting

Adhesive tank heat pads on fresh, gray, and black tanks are a common retrofit for rigs without a heated underbelly, wired to a thermostat so they only draw power when needed.

Don’t Forget the Plumbing Bay

Fresh water pumps and low-point drains are often the first thing to freeze even when tanks themselves are protected — they’re frequently outside the insulated cavity in a small unheated compartment.

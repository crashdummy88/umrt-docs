Cassette vs permanent black tank

Cassette

Carry-out cartridge, flexible for vans and small trailers, more frequent walks to a dump or cassette point. Capacity is honest and limited — plan days, not weeks.

Permanent black tank

Larger capacity, dump-station dependent, needs treatment and rinse discipline. Sensors and valves become part of ownership.

Hybrid / macerator notes

Some coaches pump out with a macerator — great when it works; jams and power issues are service calls. Text Now before forcing a stuck pump.

Build honesty

We do not invent “best toilet” rankings. Match tankage to trip length and dump access on MT · WY · ID · WA routes.

Seals and bowl seals

The blade or ball seal at the bowl keeps odor down when wet. A dry seal smells; a worn seal leaks. Keep the seal lubricated with manufacturer-approved toilet seal conditioner — not petroleum jelly unless the maker says so. Foot-pedal valves that stick often need cleaning, not a full toilet swap.

  • Check for water at the base after a long drive — loose mounting or a failed floor seal
  • Use RV-safe paper; wipes belong in the trash
  • Flush with enough water so solids do not sit under the bowl
  • Replace seals when odor persists after cleaning and treatment

Winter notes

Cassette

Empty and rinse before hard freeze; follow maker antifreeze guidance for residual water in mechanisms.

Black tank coaches

Full winterize includes toilet valve and lines. Do not leave the bowl trap wet in freezing storage.

FAQ

Composting toilets?

Valid for some builds — different maintenance and legality at camps. Outside this page’s scope; Text if you want a corridor install opinion.

Odor only when driving?

Often seal dryness or a vent path issue. Wet the seal and recheck.

Can I use household cleaners?

Avoid harsh acids and abrasives on plastic seals. Use RV toilet cleaners.

Electrical guide

RV electrical & shore-power troubleshooting

Frame shore-power, 12V drain, and converter symptoms before you start swapping parts — then know when metered mobile diagnostics beat DIY.

Field notes

Shore power problems and flickering lights are the most common calls we get. This page covers the wiring and power-supply side — for battery chemistry, capacity testing, and charging profiles, see Solar & battery troubleshooting. This guide assumes a standard 12V DC house bank (the overwhelming majority of RVs). If your coach runs a 24V or 48V inverter/charger stack (common with larger Victron MultiPlus/Quattro installs), voltages scale — confirm charge stages and fault text in VictronConnect, then use the Victron fault-code guide.

Shore power not working at the campsite

Start at the source before assuming the coach is the problem. Work this order:

  1. Check the campground pedestal breaker — it may have tripped on the prior guest.
  2. Inspect the shore-power cord for burnt or melted pins at both ends.
  3. Check the coach breaker panel — reset tripped breakers once; if one trips again, find the load or short.
  4. Test the 30/50 amp adapter — adapters corrode and fail more often than owners expect.
  5. Verify pedestal voltage. Anything sustained under about 108V AC can damage appliances and converters silently over time.

Pro tip

A surge protector with voltage monitoring is one of the best owner investments. It protects the A/C, microwave, and converter from low-voltage damage and pays for itself after one bad pedestal.

30 vs 50 amp honesty

Adapters do not invent amperage. If you need the full 50A story or dogbone limits, see 30 vs 50 amp. Undersized adapters that run hot are a fire risk, not a convenience feature.

Battery draining overnight

A bank that reads full at bedtime and dead by morning almost always means a parasitic draw — a detector, control module, or radio left wired hot; an aging battery near end of life; or a converter/inverter drawing current in standby. Finding the draw means pulling fuses one at a time while watching current on a meter, not guessing.

Owner checks first

Confirm inverter is truly off (not just “standby”). Kill aftermarket cameras, boosters, and USB hubs on ignition-hot circuits. Note anything that stays warm overnight. Photograph the fuse panel labels before you pull anything.

When resting voltage lies

A sulfated or capacity-dead lead-acid pack can look “full” at rest and collapse under a small load. Capacity testing beats resting voltage alone — see solar/battery troubleshooting for chemistry-specific guidance.

Flickering lights or dimming under load

Lights that dim when the water pump or furnace fan kicks on point to voltage drop somewhere in the circuit — usually a loose or corroded connection, not the light fixture itself.

Owner checklist
  • Check battery terminal connections for corrosion or looseness first — the most common cause
  • Inspect ground connections at the chassis and distribution panel
  • Remember a weak or sulfated battery can read normal at rest and still fail under load
  • Undersized wire on an aftermarket accessory can pull down the whole 12V bus
  • Measure voltage drop across each connection under actual load with a DVOM — that isolates resistance

Converter not charging properly

A converter that undercharges leaves the bank chronically low; one that overcharges cooks it early. Neither mode is obvious without testing. Internal converter fuses are a common failure point and are often overlooked. Multi-stage converters that never leave bulk usually indicate a charge-stage sensing fault.

What we meter

Output voltage at the converter, voltage at the battery under charge, and whether stages actually transition. Brand-specific fault text: Victron on the Victron guide; Progressive Dynamics / WFCO / Xantrex on their fault index when you have that chassis.

Converter vs inverter-charger

Factory converters and aftermarket MultiPlus-class inverter-chargers fail differently. If you upgraded power and kept the old converter in parallel, you can get two chargers fighting — classic high-voltage / ripple symptoms. See converter / inverter / charger.

Failure modes we see

Bad pedestal blamed on the coach

Low voltage or a weak breaker looks like “my A/C died.” Meter the pedestal first.

Melted dogbone

Hot adapters and corroded pins. Replace — do not tape and hope.

Shared noisy grounds

Aftermarket accessories bonded poorly pull lights and radios down together.

Invisible parasitic

Inverter standby, LP detectors, and “always on” modules. Fuse-pull method wins.

Converter fuse ignored

Internal fuses open; owners replace the whole unit.

Fire-risk DIY

Instant-trip breakers, scorch marks, burning smell — stop. Text for metered diagnostics.

FAQ

My surge protector shows low voltage — now what?

Do not run A/C and microwave on a weak pedestal. Ask the park to reset or move sites. Sustained under-voltage damages converters.

Inverter dies when the A/C kicks?

Often DC cable, lug torque, or bank capacity — not a bad inverter board. Meter at the inverter lugs under surge.

Is this a 24V/48V problem?

If you run Victron MultiPlus/Quattro at higher voltage, confirm alarms in VictronConnect and use the Victron fault-code guide. Do not apply 12V resting numbers blindly.

Can you diagnose remotely?

Text Now a photo of the pedestal meter, cord ends, and breaker panel. Many jobs still need on-site metering.

Field notes

A well-maintained roof doesn’t help if the leak is coming in around a window instead. Most window leaks trace back to the exterior sealant bead around the frame, not the window unit itself — a small gap or cracked bead lets water behind the frame where it can travel and show up as a stain well below or beside the actual window. Interior staining doesn’t reliably point straight up to the leak source.

Resealing vs. Full Window Replacement

If the glass, gasket, and frame are structurally sound, removing the old sealant bead and reapplying fresh sealant around the frame resolves most leaks without replacing the whole window.

Old Sealant Has to Come Off First

Applying new sealant over old, failed sealant rather than removing it first is a common shortcut that fails quickly — the new sealant can’t properly bond to the surface underneath.

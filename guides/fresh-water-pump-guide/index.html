Field notes

On corridor trips a pump that chatters at 2 a.m. or runs dry after a fill is usually air, a clogged inlet strainer, a slow leak, or freeze damage — not a mysterious “bad pump” from the start.

Who this is for

Right place if you

Have a pump that will not shut off, will not prime, cycles rapidly, or trips a fuse when it starts.

Maybe not yet if

Water tastes bad with good pressure — see sanitizing and filtration. No hot water only — water heater.

Priming and suction side

Won’t prime

Empty tank, air at a loose suction fitting, or a strainer full of debris. Confirm tank water, then work inlet hose and strainer before condemning the pump.

Runs, no water

Often still air or a closed valve / empty tank. Listen for cavitation; feel for suction leaks at clamps.

Strainer

Clear the inlet strainer on a schedule. Grit from unfiltered fills is a top cause of weak flow and early pump wear.

City water bypass

Know your valve positions. Pump on with city pressure fighting it confuses diagnosis.

Cycling and pressure

A pump that will not shut off usually cannot reach cutoff — open faucet, leak (even slow), or a check valve bleeding pressure back. Rapid chatter often points to a waterlogged or missing accumulator, or a pressure switch that has drifted. Fix the pressure path; replacing the pump alone often repeats the symptom.

12V amp draw basics

Demand pumps are 12V loads that spike at start. A weak battery or undersized wiring makes pumps sound sick. Check battery state of charge and fuse rating against the pump label — do not invent amp numbers. Hot connectors and melted fuse holders mean stop and text photos.

Freeze risk

  • Pumps and fittings crack when left wet in freezing weather
  • Winterize with pump and lines in mind — include the pump and lines in the winterize, not as an afterthought
  • After a freeze event, pressure-test carefully before trusting the system overnight
  • Bypass and drain water heaters per your model when winterizing

FAQ

Should I add an accumulator?

Many systems run smoother with a working accumulator. A failed one causes chatter that feels like a bad pump.

Pump runs on city water?

Usually a valve still open to the pump or a mis-plumbed tee — map your valves before buying parts.

Quiet vs loud pumps

Mounting, rubber feet, and solid bulkheads matter as much as brand marketing.

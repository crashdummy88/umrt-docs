Field notes

Reference values from NFPA 1192 (Standard on Recreational Vehicles) practice for regulator output pressure and the timed pressure-drop test. This is diagnostic reference material, not a DIY leak-test walkthrough — gas work should be performed or verified by a certified technician. ⚠ LP gas leaks and improper regulator adjustment can cause fire, explosion, or asphyxiation. This page is reference information only.

Operating Pressure

Most RV appliances are rated for a working range of roughly 10–14″ W.C., with 11″ W.C. as the standard set point. “W.C.” = inches of water column, the standard low-pressure gas measurement.

Lock-Up Pressure

When all burner valves are closed, a healthy regulator’s pressure rises slightly above the 11″ W.C.

Owner checklist
  • Connect a calibrated manometer to the system (commonly at a burner orifice with the valve closed), with all appliance valves shut and pilot lights out.
  • Pressurize the system, then let it relax down to a stable 8″ W.C. baseline reading — this settling period allows the regulator diaphragm and system volume to stabilize.
  • Isolate the manometer from the supply (close the service valve) and start a timer for a minimum of 3 minutes.
  • Read the manometer again. Any drop indicates a leak that must be located (soap-solution test at fittings) and repaired before the system is returned to service. A pressure rise usually indicates ambient temperature change or a valve that isn’t fully seated — not a leak.

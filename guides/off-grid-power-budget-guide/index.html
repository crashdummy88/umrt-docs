Field notes

Solar, battery, and inverter sizing all come from the same starting point: what you actually run, and for how long. Work it out here first. For each device: watts × hours used per day = watt-hours per day. A compressor fridge running roughly 8 of every 24 hours at 60W averages to about 480 Wh/day. Do this for every device, including small always-on loads like a CO detector or stereo standby — those add up over 24 hours.

Step 1: List Every Device and Its Daily Runtime

For each device: watts × hours used per day = watt-hours per day. A compressor fridge running roughly 8 of every 24 hours at 60W averages to about 480 Wh/day.

Step 2: Add a Realistic Buffer

Add 20–30% on top of your calculated total for inverter conversion losses, wiring losses, and battery charge/discharge inefficiency.

Step 3: Size Battery, Solar, and Inverter Together

Battery capacity should cover 1.5–2 days with zero sun. Solar wattage should recover a full day’s usage in your average available sun hours.

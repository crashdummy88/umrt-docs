12V vs. 120V RV Appliances Explained

Every RV runs both systems side by side. Knowing which is which changes what you can actually run off-grid.

Active corridor: Montana · Wyoming · Idaho · Washington — case-by-case beyond.

Field notes

Every RV runs both systems side by side. Knowing which is which changes what you can actually run off-grid. Lighting, water pump, furnace fan/control board, most vent fans, and slide/leveling systems typically run on 12V DC directly from the house battery — no inverter required, and generally lower draw. This is why these keep working even with the inverter off.

12V DC — Runs Directly Off the Battery

Lighting, water pump, furnace fan/control board, most vent fans, and slide/leveling systems typically run on 12V DC directly from the house battery — no inverter required, and generally lower draw.

120V AC — Needs Shore Power, Generator, or an Inverter

Microwave, residential-style fridge, AC unit, and standard household outlets are 120V AC — off-grid, that means running through an inverter, which draws from the battery bank far faster per watt-hour than an equivalen.

Why It Matters When Buying or Upgrading

A 12V compressor fridge draws far less off-grid than a residential 120V fridge run through an inverter, even though the 120V fridge may have a larger interior.

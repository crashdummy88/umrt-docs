RV furnace troubleshooting (Suburban & Atwood)

This is the ducted forced-air furnace — not the water heater that shares the brand name. Isolate ignition from the blower, then 12V, then LP, before you buy a board.

Suburban and Atwood forced-air furnaces need three things at once: 12V that stays above the board’s cutoff, proven airflow (sail switch), and clean LP at the burner. Most “dead furnace” calls are one of those three — not a failed heat exchanger.

Blower runs, no heat

The motor and the ignition path are separate. If the squirrel cage spins and you never get flame, stop blaming the blower.

LP first

Confirm the bottles are open, the regulator is not locked up from a prior over-pressure, and other LP appliances still light. A furnace that “tries” then quits is often starving at the burner while the cooktop still looks fine.

Sail switch

The sail switch proves air is moving before the board is allowed to spark. Dust, a warped sail, or a weak blower leaves it open. It is a common failure and a common misdiagnosis — test it, do not just jumper it and walk away.

Igniter / electrode

Carbon on the electrode or a cracked ceramic gives a weak or grounded spark. Clean carefully; replace if the ceramic is broken. Gap to the OEM spec for that board, not “close enough.”

Limit / door switch

An ajar access door or a tripped high-limit looks like a dead board. Reseat the door, reset only after you know why it tripped.

Won’t start at all

12V at the furnace

Ignition boards are voltage-sensitive. A house bank that still runs lights can be too low to attempt ignition. Measure at the furnace, not at the panel. The thermostat display will not warn you.

Thermostat path

Dead batteries in a wireless stat, a failed zone switch, or a broken thermostat wire look identical to a dead furnace. Confirm the furnace actually receives a heat call.

Door and sail

If it never cranks the blower, check the door switch and 12V feed first. If the blower never comes up, the sail switch never closes and ignition never starts.

When to stop

Gas smell, soot at the exhaust, or a cracked exchanger is not a DIY continue. Shut LP, ventilate, and book service.

Weak or uneven heat

Usually ducts and return air, not the burner. Blocked floor registers, crushed flex, or a stuffed return make the unit short-cycle on the limit or sail.

Registers and return

Clear every floor register. Confirm the return path into the furnace cabinet is open — luggage and furnace-bay clutter starve airflow.

Short-cycling

Rapid on/off with a hot cabinet points at restricted exhaust, a dirty sail, or a limit that is opening early. Do not keep resetting it.

Related systems

No flame plus a dead water heater or range is an LP supply problem. See the winterization notes if the coach just came out of storage — spiders and stale regulator lock-up show up together.

Related on docs

Pair with winterization if the coach sat. Book on Square if you want it isolated on the corridor.

Owner checklist
  • Confirm other LP appliances light before condemning the furnace
  • Measure 12V at the furnace during a heat call
  • Listen: blower only, then click/spark, then flame — note where it dies
  • Do not jumper the sail switch as a “repair”
  • If you smell gas or see soot, stop

FAQ

Is this the water heater?

No. Same brand names, different appliance. Water-heater no-heat is a different path.

Can I run it on low batteries?

You can try. The board often will not. Charge or shore-power the house bank and retry before ordering parts.

Why did it die after storage?

Sail switch dust, wasp nests in the exhaust, and regulator lock-up after bottles were swapped are the usual three.

Board swap first?

Almost never. Prove 12V, sail, and spark first. Boards get replaced after those pass.

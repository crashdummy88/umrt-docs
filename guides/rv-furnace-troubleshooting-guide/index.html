RV Furnace Troubleshooting Guide (Suburban & Atwood)

Suburban and Atwood forced-air furnaces — the ducted heating system, not the water heater sharing the same brand name.

Source: WP Field Guide. Corridor: Montana · Wyoming · Idaho · Washington — case-by-case beyond.

Field notes

Blower runs but no heat

This isolates the fault to ignition, not the blower motor. Check:

  • LP supply pressure
  • The sail switch (confirms airflow before allowing ignition — a common failure point)
  • The igniter/electrode assembly for carbon buildup or a cracked ceramic

LP symptoms that are safety-sensitive (smell of gas, hissing, detector alarm) belong on the WP LP gas troubleshooting page — shut the tank valve, evacuate, and do not operate switches until a professional leak-down.

Furnace won’t start at all

Check 12V supply first — furnace ignition boards are voltage-sensitive and won’t attempt ignition below a threshold the thermostat display may not warn you about. After that, check the thermostat’s own battery or wiring and the door/limit switch, which shuts the unit down if a service door is ajar.

From the live LP guide (linked). DSI furnaces run on 12V DC for the ignition board even though the appliance burns propane. A weak house battery produces a spark too weak to reliably ignite the burner — it looks like a “bad furnace.”

Weak or uneven heat through the ducts

Usually a duct or register obstruction rather than a furnace fault — check that nothing is blocking floor registers, and that the return air path to the furnace itself is clear. A furnace short-cycling (starting and stopping rapidly) instead points to the sail switch or a restricted exhaust, not the ductwork.

When to stop and book

Owner checks

12V at the board, thermostat, door switch, registers, and obvious LP supply (tank open, not empty).

Book when

Sail switch, electrode, or LP pressure diagnosis. Gas work is not a DIY guess. Labor $150/hr on pricing. Appliance repair starts at $150 + parts on that same page.

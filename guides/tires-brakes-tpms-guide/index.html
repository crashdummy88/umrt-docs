Chassis guide

Tires, brakes, and TPMS as one safety stack

Corridor miles punish underinflation and weak trailer brakes. Treat tires, brakes, and TPMS together — not as separate shopping lists.

Active corridor: Montana · Wyoming · Idaho · Washington — case-by-case beyond.

Field notes

Heat is the enemy. Underinflated tires and dragging brakes both cook components. A TPMS that you actually monitor is worth more than a fancy unused one.

Stack the system

Tires

Pressure for load, age inspection, matching sizes/load range. See aging and pressure-chart guides.

Brakes

Trailer electric brakes need a working controller, clean wiring, and adjusted magnets/shoes per the axle maker.

TPMS

Alerts for heat and pressure loss. Mount sensors so they survive travel; check batteries.

Before a pass

Cold pressures, controller gain check in a lot, listen for drag after a stop.

DIY vs book

DIY

Inflation, visual inspections, TPMS pairing, basic controller setup per manuals.

Book UMRT

Pulling left/right, burning smell, no trailer brakes, or repeated heat alerts — Text with TPMS screenshots.

Owner checklist
  • Set cold pressure for actual weight
  • Verify trailer brakes before highway entry
  • Run TPMS every travel day
  • Re-check after loading propane/water/cargo

Field reality on the corridor

Measure before you buy parts

Forums love parts lists. A meter, a scale ticket, or a cold pressure check usually beats a guess. Bring photos when you Text Now — year, make, and a clear shot of the fault beat a long thread.

DIY boundary

Owners can inspect, document, and do basic setup from manufacturer manuals. Stop when you smell burning, see structural damage, or the fix needs sealed systems, gas work, or high-current DC you are not trained for.

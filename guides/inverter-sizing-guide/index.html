Power guide

Size your RV inverter correctly

An undersized inverter trips constantly. An oversized one wastes standby power every hour of every day. Size it to what you actually run.

Source: WP Field Guide. Corridor: Montana · Wyoming · Idaho · Washington — case-by-case beyond.

Field notes

Continuous load vs. surge load

Every AC device draws a running wattage, but motor-driven devices (compressor fridges, microwaves, power tools, some water pumps) draw a much higher surge for the first fraction of a second on startup — often 2–3× the running wattage. Your inverter’s continuous rating needs to cover everything running at once; its surge rating needs to cover the largest single startup spike on top of that.

Pure sine vs. modified sine

Modified sine inverters are cheaper but run some electronics hot, cause audible buzz in certain devices, and can outright damage sensitive equipment like CPAP machines or variable-speed motors. Pure sine is the standard for any modern install and is what Victron and other quality inverter/chargers use. Buying notes: best inverter/chargers.

Don’t forget standby draw

A larger inverter left on 24/7 draws more idle current just staying awake, even with nothing running. If you are not running large loads constantly, an inverter with a low-power search/standby mode (most quality units have one) meaningfully extends battery life between charges.

From the live Field Guide. Size continuous to simultaneous loads, surge to the worst motor start, then check idle draw so a “bigger is safer” unit does not drain the bank overnight.

Size the RV inverter correctly

An undersized inverter trips when the microwave and the compressor fridge start together. An oversized inverter wastes standby watts every hour you leave it on. Size to the loads, then pick the box.

List the AC loads that can run at the same time. Use running watts, then add the worst startup surge (compressor fridge, microwave, some pumps and tools — often 2–3× running). The inverter’s continuous rating must cover the simultaneous running total. Its surge rating must cover the worst start on top of whatever else is already on.

Continuous vs surge

Running watts

Nameplate or a kill-a-watt at the outlet. Add everything that will be on together — not the whole coach inventory.

Surge

Motors and magnetrons spike. A 1,000 W microwave on a 1,000 W inverter is already a bad idea. Leave headroom.

Standby / idle

A large inverter left on 24/7 is a daily Wh load even with nothing plugged in. If you only need AC for an hour of cooking, use a switched or assist mode — do not leave a 3 kVA brick idling all night.

DC side

Inverter current on 12 V is brutal. Cable gauge, fuse, and busbars have to match. A correctly sized inverter on undersized cable is a fire-watch, not a power upgrade.

Waveform and charger combos

Pure sine

The default for modern coaches. CPAP, variable-speed motors, and most electronics expect it.

Modified sine

Cheaper. Buzzes some loads, heats motors, and can damage medical gear. Do not buy it to “save money” on a coach you sleep in.

Inverter/charger

Shore or generator AC charges the bank through the same box. Fewer parts than a separate converter plus inverter — if you commission it as one system.

Owner checklist
  • Write simultaneous running watts, then the worst surge
  • Include inverter idle in the daily Wh budget if it stays on
  • Size DC cable and fusing for the 12 V or 24 V current, not the AC label
  • Pure sine for CPAP and modern electronics
  • Commission transfer and charge profiles before you trust shore power

FAQ

Can I run the air conditioner?

Only if the inverter, bank, and (usually) generator math say so. Most stock 12 V banks will not.

Why does it alarm at idle?

Low battery voltage, overload, or over-temp. Read the code before you reset it.

Bigger inverter, smaller battery?

That is how you get a voltage collapse on surge. Bank and inverter are a pair.

DIY vs book?

Plug-in day-use units are owner territory. Hardwired transfer, AC panels, and Victron commissioning are the book line.

MPPT vs PWM solar charge controllers

Both keep the battery from being overcharged. Only one of them consistently turns roof voltage into battery current. Here is when the extra money for MPPT is real, and when it is not.

A PWM controller connects the panel more or less straight to the battery and clips whatever voltage is above the battery. An MPPT controller runs the array at its better power point and converts the extra volts into amps. On a small 12 V panel sitting on a 12 V bank, the gap is small. On a series roof string, or any array past a couple hundred watts, the gap is the reason you bought solar.

How they actually differ

PWM

Cheaper, simpler, fine when panel Vmp is already close to battery voltage. It cannot use extra volts. Heat and cold just throw away headroom.

MPPT

Tracks the array’s power point. Series strings, higher Voc, and cold mornings become extra charge current instead of wasted voltage.

When PWM is enough

A single ~100–150 W trickle panel on a 12 V bank, weekend use, no series wiring. The efficiency loss is real and small at that scale.

When MPPT is the buy

Anything over roughly 200–300 W, any series string, lithium banks you actually cycle, or a roof you cannot expand later.

Cold weather is the MPPT case

Panel voltage rises as temperature drops. A PWM unit cannot spend those extra volts. An MPPT unit can, which is why winter corridor mornings are where the controller choice shows up on the shunt — not in a July driveway test.

Voc headroom

Size the controller for cold Voc, not sticker Voc. A “100 V” MPPT that is fine in Arizona can over-voltage in a Montana dawn.

Current rating

MPPT output current is battery-side. A 30 A controller on a 12 V bank is ~400 W class, not “whatever the panels say.”

What we spec

Owner checklist
  • Write array Voc (cold) and Imp before picking a controller
  • Do not series-string into PWM
  • Match lithium vs AGM charge profile in the app
  • Fuse array and battery sides
  • Confirm the monitor shows expected amps at solar noon once

FAQ

Can I “upgrade later” from PWM?

Yes, but you may also need different string wiring. Plan the roof for the controller you will keep.

Two cheap PWMs vs one MPPT?

Usually one correctly sized MPPT. Split arrays only when shade or roof geometry forces it.

Why is my MPPT sitting at a few amps?

Shade, dirty glass, a full bank already in float, or a voltage window that does not match the string.

Victron required?

No. It is what we commission. Any MPPT with an honest voltage window and a chemistry profile you can set is the idea.
